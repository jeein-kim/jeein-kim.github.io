About Me

I am a PhD student at Seoul University, working on how star formation is regulated in galaxies. I am broadly interested in understanding how stars form from dense molecular gas, and how this process is connected to the broader matter cycle and evolution of galaxies. My work is driven by observations across the electromagnetic spectrum — from GALEX (UV) and the VLT (optical) to JWST (infrared) and ALMA (radio) — combining these to build a comprehensive picture of star formation, from the Milky Way to nearby galaxies.

Research

Studying star formation and the dense molecular gas that fuels it,
from the Milky Way to nearby galaxies.

  • The ALMOND Survey

    Mapping the largest sample of nearby galaxies in dense gas tracers with ALMA, and connecting star formation in the dense gas to the properties of molecular clouds across 25 spiral galaxies.

  • M100 (NGC 4321)

    A deep, high-resolution study of a Milky-Way analog combining ALMA, MUSE and JWST, revealing how dense gas and star formation efficiency vary across the galaxy's disk, bar and center.

  • The LEGO Survey

    Mapping star-forming regions in the Milky Way with "extragalactic eyes," using the IRAM 30m telescope to connect Galactic and extragalactic studies of star formation.
