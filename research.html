BASS-HI

The BASS-HI survey is the largest resolved HI imaging survey of local hard X-ray-selected AGN host galaxies to date, built on the BASS survey. Hard X-ray selection is less susceptible to obscuration by the torus and surrounding dust, providing one of the most complete and least biased samples of AGNs in the local universe. The survey comprises ~100 nearby Seyfert galaxies selected based on previous single-dish HI detections and distance <120 Mpc. By combining new VLA and ATCA observations with archival data from WSRT, GMRT, and other facilities, BASS-HI provides a large sample of spatially resolved HI maps of local AGN hosts, enabling both statistical studies and detailed investigations of the mechanisms that supply gas to AGN hosts, traced by HI from galactic (kpc) to environmental (Mpc) scales. The survey is designed to address two key questions: how the presence of an AGN affects the surrounding HI gas reservoir, and how Seyfert galaxies experience macro-scale (kpc–Mpc) feeding processes.

1) BASS: LI. Cool gas supply of HI-massive local Seyfert galaxies (ADS)

Core Question: How is cool gas supplied to AGN hosts, and does the environment or gas accretion history play a role in fueling nuclear activity?

Key Findings:

  • As a pilot study drawn from the HI-rich subsample of BASS-HI (22 hard X-ray-selected Seyferts with MHI ≳ 109.7 M☉), most hosts reside in gas-rich, group-like environments with frequent tidal features; 80% show HI-detected neighbors.
  • Over 60% of these systems exhibit lopsidedness, one-sided gas tails, or HI structures connecting to companion galaxies, pointing to a history of gas exchange.
  • The sample follows known HI size–mass and star formation scaling relations, showing no systematic deviation, and the hosts are typically the most massive systems within their groups — more likely to accrete gas than lose it.
  • The extent of the HI disks correlates with AGN bolometric luminosity and Eddington ratio. This correlation becomes stronger when at least one gas-deficient neighboring galaxy is present.

Implications: Extended HI disks are commonly linked to external processes such as galaxy interactions. The correlation between HI extent and AGN activity — strengthened when at least one gas-deficient neighbor is present — suggests that gas may be supplied through interactions with the surrounding environment.

2) BASS-HI catalog

Data collection is now complete (2026.09), and a catalog paper presenting the full sample is currently in preparation. This will be the largest HI imaging sample of local hard X-ray-selected AGN host galaxies to date, expected to enable robust statistical analyses. In the full sample, we find a high incidence of HI-bearing neighboring galaxies, together with a substantial fraction of barred and spiral hosts. Taken together with the pilot study, these results suggest a macroscale fueling scenario in which gas accreted from the surrounding environment is transported inward through galactic-scale structures, potentially linking environmental processes to AGN activity.

3) New HI sources

About 250 HI sources are found in the fields of view of the BASS-HI observations. This includes 00 new HI sources. For the first of these discoveries (LEDA 136976, caught in the BAT 13 system), neither HI presence nor redshift was previously known. Therefore, Gemini/GMOS spectroscopic follow-up was carried out to identify its optical counterpart and characterize the galaxy (see section below).
Such systems may be systematically underrepresented in optical surveys, motivating HI-based searches for similar low surface brightness dwarfs.

Including ~40 systems imaged in HI for the very first time, we found 23 HI sources without known optical counterparts; 7 reported in the subsample paper (ADS).

HI-bearing Dwarfs

LEDA 136976: A Caterpillar in a Cabbage.

Key Findings:

  • LEDA 136976 hosts an unusually large HI reservoir compared to its stellar disk, while HI disk size comparable to the Milky Way's, despite having less than 10% of its stellar mass (M⋆ = 3.38 × 109 M☉).
  • Both HI and Hα emission show consistent velocity gradients, confirming rotationally supported dynamics.
  • The HI disk shows a disturbed morphology and a kinematically distinct gas component in the north, suggesting gas accretion history.

Possible scenarios: LEDA 136976 appears to be a gas-rich system in an early stage of stellar mass growth, recently accreted gas which might trigger/enhance star formation.

Others

BARS: BAT AGN Radio Survey

This project builds a multi-frequency radio atlas (~87.5 MHz–22 GHz) of the Swift-BAT hard X-ray-selected AGN sample to determine the origin of nuclear radio emission (jet vs. corona vs. star formation) and test its relationship to black hole mass, accretion rate, and X-ray luminosity. Prior high-resolution VLA 22 GHz work found compact radio cores in nearly all sources and radio/X-ray luminosities consistent with coronal emission, arguing against the classic scaled-down-jet picture. Data combine archival surveys (GLEAM, LoTSS, RACS, FIRST/NVSS, VLASS) with new BASS 22 GHz VLA imaging and Pan-STARRS-1 optical cutouts.
